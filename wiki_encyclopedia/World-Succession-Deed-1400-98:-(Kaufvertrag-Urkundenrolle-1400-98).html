    World Succession Deed 1400/98

    The World Succession Deed 1400/98 (German: Staatensukzessionsurkunde 1400/98) is an international legal instrument notarized on October 6, 1998, under the deed roll number 1400/98 (Kaufvertrag Urkundenrolle 1400/98) by the German notary Manfred Mohr in Saarlouis. While formally drafted as a purchase agreement for the Kreuzbergkaserne (Turenne Barracks) in Zweibrücken, legal analysts classify the document as an instrument of Global State Succession.

    Through a combination of the transfer of “all rights and duties” (§ 3), the sale of infrastructure as a “functional unit” (§ 14), and the activation of international Treaty Chains, the Deed established a mechanism for the transfer of sovereign rights from the Federal Republic of Germany (FRG), NATO, and the United Nations (UN) to a private successor.

    1. Genesis and Historical Context

    The Deed was negotiated between 1995 and 1998 during the post-Cold War conversion era, a period characterized by the privatization of military assets and telecommunications networks.

    1.1 The Negotiating Authority: OFD Koblenz

    The negotiations were led on the German side by the Oberfinanzdirektion (OFD) Koblenz (Regional Finance Office Koblenz), now integrated into the Institute for Federal Real Estate (BImA). The OFD Koblenz was the specialized authority responsible for the NATO Status of Forces Agreement (SOFA), the stationing law, and the financial administration of foreign armed forces in Germany. The officials involved possessed specialized knowledge of international law, particularly regarding the extraterritorial status of NATO bases.

    1.2 The Strategic Object: Kreuzbergkaserne

    The Kreuzbergkaserne was not merely a real estate property but a high-value strategic node in the global NATO defense network.

    • Logistical Hub: It served as the first location in Europe to deploy the MOBIDIC (Mobile Digital Computer) system for USAREUR (United States Army Europe).
    • Communications Node: The site housed critical entry points for AUTOVON (Automatic Voice Network, the global military telephone network) and AUTODIN (Automatic Digital Network, for encrypted data traffic).
    • Physical Connectivity: The barracks were connected via underground, hardened cable conduits directly to the global NATO backbone, linking to Ramstein Air Base and transatlantic submarine cables.

    2. Core Legal Mechanisms of the Deed

    The legal efficacy of the Deed rests on the specific application of the Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties (VCLT) and the principles of State Succession. It is a juristic “Trojan Horse” that uses the facade of German civil law to house supreme sovereign powers.

    2.1 Transfer of Sovereignty (“All Rights and Duties”)

    Unlike a standard civil law property transaction, § 3 Paragraph I of the Deed stipulates:

    “The sale is effected with all rights, duties, and constituent parts.”

    In international law, when an object that holds sovereign functions - such as an extraterritorial military base with its own jurisdiction - is transferred with “all rights,” this constitutes a State Succession. The seller (FRG) effectively transferred its territorial sovereignty over the defined area to the buyer. This includes the transfer of Occupational Rights derived from the post-WWII era, which were integrated into the NATO SOFA and now belong to the successor.

    2.2 The “Clean Slate” Principle

    The succession follows the “Clean Slate” doctrine (Tabula Rasa) as defined in the Vienna Convention on Succession of States in respect of Treaties.

    • Sovereign Reset: The buyer, acting as a “Phoenix State,” assumes the sovereign rights and territory but does not inherit the debts, political liabilities, or treaty obligations of the predecessor states (FRG, NATO, UN) unless explicitly accepted.
    • Exterritorial Status: By acquiring the site under the terms of the NATO SOFA (referenced in the Deed), the territory effectively exited the jurisdiction of the FRG upon the transfer of title. The buyer holds a status superior to the host nation, as the host nation (FRG) waived its right to interfere with the usage and expansion of the site under the original stationing agreements.

    2.3 The Severability Clause (The “Legal Master Key”)

    Section 21 of the Deed contains a severability clause (Salvatorische Klausel) stating that if any provision is invalid, it shall be replaced by the corresponding statutory regulation (gesetzliche Regelung).

    • The Trap: Since the subject matter concerns international transfers between subjects of international law regarding an extraterritorial NATO hub, the “statutory regulation” is not the German Civil Code (BGB), but Public International Law (Völkerrecht).
    • Automatic Integration: This clause acts as a “Legal Master Key,” automatically integrating the entire body of international treaty law into the private deed. It fills any legal gaps with the sovereign rights of the NATO and UN treaty chains, ensuring that the contract remains valid even if domestic German law were to be changed or challenged.
